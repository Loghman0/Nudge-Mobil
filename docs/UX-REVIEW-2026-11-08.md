# Native Nudge: parity, usability and accessibility review

8 November 2026. This review began from the 0.2 source. Implementation may advance during the same session; [PARITY.md](PARITY.md) remains the feature-status ledger.

**Reading this as:** a self-discovery app that turns light answers into inspectable reflections and later helps with choices, for people who want to understand their preferences, with a calm, curious language and Nudge's existing blue identity.

Design variance 6/10; motion 4/10; density 4/10. The lower motion/density are proposals that leave room for questions and large text. They are not measured user preferences. The stack and Android-first direction are already selected; this review does not restart them or replace the product with a mood tracker.

## Practical evidence-to-design criteria

Android recommends at least 48 dp touch targets, suitable contrast and purpose-based labels. Nudge's current `layout.target=48` is a source fact; measuring actual rendered targets and TalkBack order remains necessary. Small normal text should meet 4.5:1, with non-color selected/error cues. [Android accessibility guidance](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps).

Use one clear question at a time, a visible way back, valid uncertain answers and short contextual hints. Reuse information instead of asking again. This is an application of an established service-design pattern, not evidence of higher Nudge retention. [GOV.UK question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/).

Expose the main action first and name secondary disclosures by their contents. Advanced controls must stay discoverable; hiding deletion, consent or uncertainty to improve apparent simplicity is unacceptable. [Jakob Nielsen on progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/).

Display pending/saved/error status and a recovery action. Announce relevant state changes without unnecessarily stealing focus. Describe the invalid field and a concrete correction in text; color alone is insufficient. [W3C status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html), [W3C error identification](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-identification.html).

Keep terminology consistent across web/native, retain Back/Cancel, make evidence recognizable and avoid forcing users to remember earlier values. These are heuristic review criteria, not automatic proof of usability. [Nielsen's own usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/).

Avoid obstructive exit/consent patterns, preselected data sharing, hidden costs or attention traps. Nudge has no evidence that guilt-based streaks or artificial scarcity help its users. A variable question bank may provide variety; do not turn it into an endless, coercive reward loop. [FTC dark-pattern report](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light).

## Psychology terms from the brief: concrete use and misuse checks

The table translates the brief's terms into **proposed Nudge design decisions**. It does not claim that a named effect predicts a particular user's behavior. Original historical papers for several terms were not retrievable in this pass; no new empirical findings or universal thresholds are asserted from them.

| Principle / heuristic | Nudge application | Benefit to test | Misuse / pushback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fitts / thumb reach | Large full-label answer targets; easy-to-reach Continue; no tiny delete × as the only control. | Fewer accidental selections across real phone sizes. | Enlarging a destructive or paid action to bias selection. Thumb reach is device/hand dependent. |
| Hick / choice complexity | Today starts with a question; topic selection is a secondary chooser; Decide stages option meaning then criteria. | Users can find the next action without reading every control. | Removing honest “depends/neither” choices merely to reduce option count. |
| Jakob / familiarity | Consistent native Back, tabs, sheets and labels; Nudge retains its own loop. | Predictable navigation without a tutorial. | Copying a social feed or competitor visual identity. |
| Miller / memory load | Carry forward selected answers; show option meanings beside relevant criteria. | Users need not memorize a previous screen. | Treating “7±2” as a universal menu-size rule or clinical assessment. |
| Doherty / response feedback | Immediate pressed/selected state; visible pending save while the server works; measure latency. | Users can distinguish a tap from a lost action. | Pretending durable save succeeded to meet a numerical response target. No 400 ms performance pass is claimed. |
| Aesthetic-usability effect | Clean hierarchy and consistent blue tokens, backed by real accessibility/error checks. | People can read and act without decorative competition. | Assuming attractive screenshots establish working or safe behavior. |
| Peak-end | End the six-question preview with a small grounded reflection and a clear choice to save/leave. | The session has an understandable, useful ending. | Inflated praise or false personality certainty for a memorable finish. |
| Zeigarnik / unfinished tasks | Restore a deliberate draft and label its state. | An interruption need not force re-entry. | Badges or warnings that create pressure to finish optional questions. |
| Defaults | Easy depth, AI/optional data sharing off unless explicitly chosen. | Low-pressure entry; sensitive topics remain a choice. | Defaulting consent, reminders or clinical interpretations on. |
| Loss aversion | State deletion consequences accurately and allow Cancel. | Fewer irreversible mistakes. | “Don't lose your streak/insight” copy used to prevent exit. |
| Von Restorff / isolation | One primary action and one selected-answer indicator. | Important actions can be located. | Several equally loud accents or attention-grabbing permission prompts. |
| Progressive disclosure | Sources, settings and journal details expand when requested. | Core question stays readable while depth remains available. | Hiding essential limitations or making privacy controls hard to reach. |
| Social proof | None needed for core answering; only real, permissioned evidence if later introduced. | Avoids invented endorsement pressure. | Fake user counts/testimonials or presenting a circle majority as personal truth. |
| Reciprocity | Useful local reflection before signup; optional save. | People can judge value before giving account data. | Making the free preview feel like a debt to register. |
| Variable rewards / novelty | Diverse questions chosen with fatigue limits and a visible stopping point. | Variety may reduce repetitiveness; test whether people want it. | Infinite answer loops, gambling-like surprise rewards or notification pressure. |
| Emotional design | Curious, specific, nonjudgmental answer wording and editable reflections. | Users can disagree without being labeled. | Inferring aggression/diagnosis from a single imaginative answer. |

## Learning from actual people after the expert walkthrough

Recruit 5–8 participants across first-time/returning use and access needs as a proposed formative study, not a statistical sample. Do not invent interview results. Ask people to try the P0 journeys without coaching, narrate uncertainties, and explain one reflection in their own words. Record task success, mistaken taps, recovery, time to first answer and where they stop; separate observation from the reviewer's interpretation. Ask whether the reflection fits and what evidence they think it used. “Would you answer another?” is a preference report, not actual retention.

Existing analytics/support evidence was not supplied to this reviewer. Optional metrics should use event counts and timings, never raw answer/journal text. Measure onboarding completion, voluntary save conversion, return sessions, insight-fit feedback, error/retry/conflict rates, and successful edit/exclude/delete use. More answers alone is not a success criterion: voluntary stopping, low burden and trust also matter. No numerical improvement target is claimed before a baseline exists.
